The 402

A self-discovery app for Omaha — events, places and things to do, built for people who already live here rather than for visitors.

THE 402 — HOME SCREEN
01

The question

Every city app is built for visitors. They rank the same twelve restaurants, they assume you have never been here, and they are useless by your second week in town. The question behind The 402 was whether an app could be built for the opposite person — someone who lives in Omaha and has run out of ideas.

That reframing changed almost every decision. No onboarding tour. No "top attractions". No map as the home screen, because a resident already knows where things are — what they lack is a reason to go.

02
If you already live somewhere, discovery is not a map problem. It is a boredom problem.

The home screen became a single prompt rather than a directory: one thing, chosen for the day and the weather, with the option to ask for another. Everything else — search, saved places, the full listings — sits behind that one decision.

03

What it took

The hard part was not the interface, it was the data. Omaha has no clean events API, so listings are assembled from a handful of sources and corrected by hand weekly — which is sustainable at one city and would not be at ten. That constraint is deliberate for now.

Built in SwiftUI with a small Postgres backend. The recommendation logic is intentionally dumb: recency, weather, distance, and whether you have already been shown it this week. Nothing learns, nothing profiles, nothing needs an account.

04

Where it is now

Live, and used mostly by people who know the studio. The next question is whether the weekly hand-correction can be automated without the listings getting worse — which is the kind of thing that starts as a Playground experiment before it comes back here.
